# Worked examples

Two finished sheets, to show the format at a useful level of detail. They are illustrations of two very different styles, filled from earlier work in one product. They are not defaults: re-derive every value for a new style by researching it (step 2 of the skill), and expect a new style to differ in most fields.

## An ambient score for space and sleep

- **Identity.** Slow, unresolved, spacious; neighbours: new age, drone, film pads.
- **Pulse and feel.** Slow and straight, no swing; tempo is felt in long bars rather than counted.
- **Harmony.** A bright, floating mode; open chords with the third left out of some; chords last many bars; no strong cadence, because an endless piece should not end.
- **Rhythm section.** None, or a very soft pulse.
- **Voices.** A pad bed with a slow swell, a sub drone, and soft bell tones from a note pool that cannot clash; all kept low and dark.
- **The idea.** No hook. A few slow gestures and a drifting pool of notes; a rare colour note.
- **Form.** A piece of a couple of minutes, sparse to fuller and back; pieces change key by a small step.
- **Space and mix.** A long dark reverb on the beds with its low end removed; very little energy in the bright band; quiet.
- **Variation over time.** Several layers on lengths that do not divide the bar, a filter that drifts on cycles that do not line up, a rare event every few minutes.
- **Listener risks.** A shrill sustained note, a muddy tail, a loop the ear learns.

## A smooth house track for a background listener

- **Identity.** A warm, soft take on dance music of the nineties, to be left on; neighbours: loud club house, lo-fi.
- **Pulse and feel.** A steady four-to-the-floor at a comfortable dance tempo, a light shuffle on the off sixteenths, timing a few milliseconds loose.
- **Harmony.** Minor-key sevenths and ninths, a chord to a bar or two, a short progression that loops; a round sub bass on the offbeats.
- **Rhythm section.** A soft kick on every beat, a quiet clap on two and four, open hats on the offbeats, closed hats whispered between them, a faint ghost-snare figure on the late sixteenths for a touch of jungle.
- **Voices.** Warm electric-piano stabs as the riff, a swelling pad, the sub, a little vinyl crackle. No lead, no bells.
- **The idea.** The stab rhythm, chosen once per piece and locked on the grid.
- **Form.** Intro, loop, loop, a breakdown without the kick, loop, outro, in four- and eight-bar blocks; the kick keeps going across the join so the loop never drops out.
- **Space and mix.** A short dark room on the pad and stabs only, never the drums or bass; a low-pass that makes it unobtrusive; quiet.
- **Variation over time.** The groove is fixed. It changes at the end of every fourth bar (a turnaround), at section changes and in the breakdown; a busy page adds a stab.
- **Listener risks.** A loud kick, bright stabs, busy hats, anything that pulls the ear.

## What the two show

The sheets disagree on almost every field: the first rests and drifts, the second locks and turns around. That is the point. A style's tells decide whether a layer should be free or fixed, so write the tells down before building.
